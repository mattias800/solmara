## Plug in

1. Choose your interface in **Preferences › Audio**, under **Audio input**.
2. Make an **audio track** (<kbd>Cmd/Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd>), right-click its arm key and choose the input channel your guitar is on, the left or the right.
3. Set the arm key's menu to **hear the input through the track** while armed, so you play through its effects.
4. Arm the track. You now hear your guitar through whatever is in its chain.

Keep the block size in **Preferences › Audio** small, so what you play is heard at once. On Windows, **Take it for Solmara alone** gives the lowest latency.

## Build a rig

The library's **Audio effects › Guitar & bass** has its devices in the order the sound goes through them: **Pedals**, **Guitar amps**, **Bass amps** and **Cabs**. A rig is built the same way, left to right in the chain:

1. **Pedals:** a gate, a compressor, overdrives, fuzzes and distortions, a wah, then modulation and delays.
2. **An amp.** Most amps are the amp alone, without a speaker: the line under their knobs names the cabinet to put after them.
3. **A cabinet**, the miked speaker box. **Boxed Twelves** has cabinets of its own; **Cabinet IR** plays an impulse response you load.
4. **Effects after the amp:** reverb, delay, and an EQ.

The **rigs**, such as **Leviathan Rig**, **Djent Amp** and **Untitled Amp**, are a whole chain in one device: gate, drives, amps, EQ and cabinet, each part switched on and off by itself.

Amp and pedal faces have a **Quality** switch: how far above the sample rate the circuit is worked out. Higher sounds smoother when driven hard and costs more processing; **Light** is the cheapest.

## Captured tones from TONE3000

**A2 Modeller Amp** plays tones captured with Neural Amp Modeler: an amp, a pedal or a whole rig, as someone recorded it.

1. Open **View › Get content…** and choose **TONE3000**. Sign in from there.
2. Search for gear or a sound, listen, and download the tones you like into your library.
3. In the library, **Tones** lists them by gear, with their picture and who made them. Double-click one to play it on the track's A2 Modeller Amp.

A project keeps the tones it uses, so it opens and sounds the same on another computer. **Normalize** levels each tone, so switching tones does not jump in loudness.

## Tune up

**Tuner**, among the pedals, shows the note you hold and how many cents it is off, against your reference A, and can mute the sound while you tune.

## Make a DI sound like a guitar you want

**Guitar Auto-EQ** turns your guitar's tone toward another. Press **Listen** and play for five seconds so it learns your guitar; it then equalises toward a clean passive-pickup DI, or toward a **tone profile**, another guitar it learned and you kept in the library. **Amount** says how far it goes, and **Smooth** how much of your guitar's own detail it keeps. Put it before the amp.

## Bass

The **Bass amps**, a flip-top combo, a 300-watt stack and a hybrid head, take a bass as the guitar amps take a guitar. **Mono Bass**, among the utilities, keeps the lows in mono.
