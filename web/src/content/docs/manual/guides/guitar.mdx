## Plug in

1. Choose your interface in **Preferences › Audio**, under **Input and recording**.
2. Make an **audio track** (<kbd>Cmd/Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd>) and right-click its arm key. Under **Takes of the input**, choose the channel your guitar is on, under **Mono, on both sides**.
3. In the same menu, set **Hear the input through the track** to **While it is armed**, so you play through its effects.
4. Arm the track. You now hear your guitar through whatever is in its chain.

Keep the block size in **Preferences › Audio** small, so what you play is heard at once. On Windows, **Exclusive mode** gives the lowest latency.

## Build a rig

The library's **Audio effects › Guitar & bass** has its devices in the order the sound goes through them: **Pedals**, **Guitar amps**, **Bass amps** and **Cabs**. A rig is built the same way, left to right in the chain:

1. **Pedals:** a gate, a compressor, overdrives, fuzzes and distortions, a wah, then modulation and delays.
2. **An amp.** Most amps are the amp alone, without a speaker: the line under their knobs names the cabinet to put after them.
3. **A cabinet**, the miked speaker box. **Boxed Twelves** has cabinets of its own; **Cabinet IR** plays an impulse response you load.
4. **Effects after the amp:** reverb, delay, and an EQ.

The **rigs**, **Leviathan Rig**, **Djent Amp**, **Untitled Amp** and **Chug Rig**, are a whole chain in one device: gate, drives, amps, EQ and cabinet, each part switched on and off by itself.

Most amps and pedals have a **Quality** switch, such as **Light** and **Standard**, or **Standard** and **Ultra**: how finely the circuit is worked out. The higher setting sounds smoother when driven hard and costs more processing.

## Captured tones from TONE3000

**A2 Modeler Amp** plays tones captured with Neural Amp Modeler: an amp, a pedal or a whole rig, as someone recorded it.

1. In the library, open **Tones** and click **Find tones on TONE3000…**. Sign in to TONE3000 there.
2. Search for gear or a sound. The tones you pick are downloaded into your library.
3. **Tones** lists them, with their picture and who made them. Double-click one to play it on the selected track's A2 Modeler Amp, or drag it to a track or a chain.

A project keeps the tones it uses, so it opens and sounds the same on another computer. **Normalize** levels each tone, so switching tones does not jump in loudness.

## Tune up

**Tuner**, among the pedals, shows the note you hold and how many cents it is off, against your reference A, and can mute the sound while you tune.

## Make a DI sound like a guitar you want

**Guitar Auto-EQ** turns your guitar's tone toward another. Press **Listen** and play for five seconds so it learns your guitar; it then equalizes toward a clean passive-pickup DI, or toward a **tone profile**, another guitar it learned and you kept in the library. **Amount** says how far it goes, and **Smooth** how much of your guitar's own detail it keeps. Put it before the amp.

## Bass

The **Bass amps**, **Flipside Bass Combo** (a 1960s flip-top), **Icebox Bass Stack** (300 watts into eight tens) and **Halfglow Bass Head** (a hybrid), take a bass as the guitar amps take a guitar, each with its cabinet. **Mono Bass**, among the utilities, keeps the lows in mono.
