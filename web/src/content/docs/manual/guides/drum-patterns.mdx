Besides plain clips of notes, a MIDI track can hold two kinds of **pattern**, written the way drum machines and bass machines are programmed: in steps.

## Make a pattern

Right-click an empty place on a MIDI track and choose **Create Drum pattern** or **Create Step pattern**. A clip of notes on a drum track can also become a drum pattern: **Convert to drum pattern** in its menu. Either kind turns back into plain notes with **To MIDI**.

## The drum editor

<Shot src={drums} alt="The drum editor: a lane for each drum, sixteen steps across, the accent row above them." />

A **lane** for each drum, sixteen **steps** across, a page at a time.

- **Click a step** to strike it or clear it; drag across steps to paint.
- **Drag a hit up or down** for its loudness (<kbd>Shift</kbd> for finer). <kbd>Shift</kbd>-click steps it through soft, normal and strong.
- **<kbd>Alt</kbd>-drag a hit sideways** to move it off its step, for a pushed or laid-back feel.
- **Right-click a step** for its velocity, a flam, a ratchet (a roll within the step) and its probability.
- **The accent row** above the steps accents a whole step for every drum. Right-click it to accent every beat or none.
- **A lane's head** has its drum's color and name, the key it plays, and **M** and **S**. Right-click it to rename, re-key, lengthen, move or remove it. **+ Lane** adds a drum.

The title row sets the number of steps (1 to 64), the step length, the **swing** and the groove. **Values** shows a lane of one setting under the pads (velocity, chance, ratchet, flam or offset), so you can draw a crescendo or a run of rolls across it.

**Window**, or a double-click on the clip, opens the pattern in a larger window with the library's drum patterns as cards below it. Click a card to hear it on your kit, in time while the song plays; double-click to load it, the whole pattern or one lane of it. **Save** keeps your pattern in the library.

## The step editor

A step pattern is a piano roll held to steps, for basslines, acid lines and riffs: a column is a step, a row a semitone.

- **Click a cell** to put the step's note there; drag a note up or down for its pitch (<kbd>Shift</kbd> by octaves). Double-click a note to rest its step.
- **The accent and slide lanes** under the grid: click to flip a step, or drag to paint. A slide bends into the next note.
- **Click a key at the left** to write that note into the chosen step and move on to the next, or play the steps in from the computer keyboard.
- **With the grid clicked last:** <kbd>Left</kbd> and <kbd>Right</kbd> choose a step, <kbd>Up</kbd> and <kbd>Down</kbd> move its note, and <kbd>Delete</kbd> (<kbd>Backspace</kbd> on a Mac) rests it.
- **Right-click a step** to make it a note, a tie or a rest, accent or slide it, or shift, reverse or transpose the whole pattern.

The title row has the number of steps, the step length, the swing and the gate. Every change made while the song plays is heard from the next note on.

## Drum machines

Solmara's drum machines, **PH-8**, **PH-9** and **PH-6**, play a pattern on their own kits. The **Drum Rack** builds a kit of your own a sound at a time: drop an instrument, a sample or a preset on each of its pads. A pad can choke others, as a closed hi-hat cuts an open one.
