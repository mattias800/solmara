**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the 1960s British 30-watt combo with its treble-boost channel.

A modelled British 30-watt guitar combo of the early 1960s with its treble boost, without a cabinet: a first valve whose two halves are the normal and the brilliant channels, the brilliant channel's small capacitor that passes the top, the treble boost's stage and cathode follower driving a treble and bass network, and four small European output pentodes in push-pull with no master and no feedback, biased by their shared cathode resistor, with the cut between their grids and a valve rectifier that sags. Normal and brilliant volumes, treble, bass, and cut, which jack is played or both joined, the treble boost in or out, a valve or a stiff supply, and a quality switch for how far above the sample rate the circuit is solved. It wants a cabinet after it, the speaker cabinet device. Modelled on the chiming class-A combo of the British beat groups, with its treble boost.

## Presets

8 factory presets come with British Chime Combo. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Bright Strum**: The treble up and the bass down, the volume low, the supply stiff: strummed chords that cut through a band without breaking up. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Chime**: The brilliant channel through the treble boost with its volume low: clean, bright chords that ring, and a little bite when dug into. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Cranked Thirty**: The brilliant volume at its top, so the power stage clips and the supply sags: thick, compressed chords with the cut halfway to tame the top. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Dark Lead**: The volume high, the treble down, and the cut well up: a round, singing lead with the chime taken off. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Edge of Breakup**: The treble boost with the volume just past noon: chords that clean up with the guitar's volume and grind when hit hard. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Jangle**: The brilliant channel with the treble boost out, as the combo was before it: thin and glassy, for arpeggios and twelve strings. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Joined Channels**: Both jacks joined, as a short cord across them does: the normal channel's body under the treble boost's chime. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Normal Channel**: The normal channel, full and dark beside the brilliant one, with its volume at noon: warm rhythm that starts to break up when hit. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
