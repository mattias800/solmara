**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the late-1970s Japanese green overdrive pedal.

A modelled overdrive pedal that lifts the middle of a guitar's band: an operational amplifier with two diodes back to back in its feedback, which clips softly and only what lies above the lows, so the low end passes clean and stays tight, then a tone control and a level. Drive, tone, and level, with a quality switch for how far above the sample rate the circuits are solved. With the drive low and the level high it is the boost put in front of a high-gain amplifier to tighten rhythm playing. Modelled on the small green overdrive pedal of the late 1970s, named for making a valve amplifier scream, the usual pedal before a high-gain head.

## Presets

8 factory presets come with Green Overdrive Pedal. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Bright Boost**: The tight boost with the tone well up: some 3 dB more from 700 Hz upward, for a dark guitar or an amplifier with the treble held back. Put it before the amplifier.
- **Dark and Thick**: The drive past its middle and the tone all the way down: the hump moves down to 530 Hz and 5 dB comes off between 1.5 and 3 kHz, for a smooth, woolly overdrive under a voice.
- **Full Drive**: The drive at its top and the tone just under its middle: as much as the pedal clips, 28 dB up at 800 Hz on a quiet signal and held to a diode's drop on a loud one.
- **Gentle Push**: The drive off and the level at three quarters: 12 dB in the middle and nothing at 80 Hz, enough to tip an amplifier on the edge of breaking up over it when the strings are hit.
- **Low Crunch**: The drive a third up: chords that are nearly clean played softly and gritty when dug into, with the lows left clean under them.
- **Noon**: The drive and the tone at their middle: the pedal's own overdrive, soft clipping with the middle 18 dB up and the top rolled off above it.
- **Singing Lead**: The drive well up and the tone a little past its middle, for single notes: a note played softly comes out nearly as loud as one played hard, and cuts through in the middle.
- **Tight Boost**: The drive nearly off and the level at its top, as the pedal is used before a high-gain amplifier: 16 dB more at 800 Hz and under 4 at 80, so the amplifier is driven harder in the middle and hardly harder in the lows, which tightens muted rhythm playing. Put it before the amplifier.
