**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the 1990s American high-gain valve head with its switchable rectifiers.

A modelled high-gain guitar amplifier, without a cabinet: stacked valve stages that hand their clipping on to each other, a tone stack, and a push-pull power stage on a speaker's load. Gain, bass, mid, treble, presence, and master, a clean channel and a lead one, a mode switch that takes the feedback off the power stage, a supply held stiff or rectified by silicon or by valves, which sag under a loud note, at the full voltage or a lowered one, and a quality switch for how far above the sample rate the circuit is solved. It wants a cabinet after it, the speaker cabinet device: alone it is harsh. Modelled on the two-channel American high-gain valve head of the 1990s with a choice of rectifiers, the sound of that decade's heavy guitars.

## Presets

11 factory presets come with Wreck Stack. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Clean, Bright**: The clean channel in Modern with the gain at a third and the treble and presence up: a clear top for picked notes and funk chords, with the speaker's load left on the lows. Its peaks stand high over its level, as a clean guitar's do. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Clean, Pushed**: The clean channel in Vintage with the gain past its middle: a full, loose crunch with its lows kept, where the lead channel would be tight and bright. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Clean, Warm**: The clean channel in Raw with the gain under its middle and the master at its top: full lows and a soft top that stays clean under chords and rounds off when a string is hit hard. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Crunch, Raw**: Raw, with the gain under its middle: chords that are gritty played softly and break up when they are hit. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Edge of Breakup**: Raw, with the gain low and the master up: clean when played softly and gritty when dug into. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Full Gain**: Modern, with the gain nearly at its top and the presence held back: the most lows and the least top of the modern ones, and a soft note comes out as loud as a hard one. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Lead, Singing**: Vintage, with the gain and the mid well up and the presence at its top, for single notes: one played softly comes out as loud as one played hard. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Scooped Rhythm**: Modern, with the mid at 0 and the bass and treble up: some 5 dB less around 500 Hz, for heavy chords under a band. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Thick and Dark**: Vintage, with the treble down and the presence at its middle: full lows and a soft top, for slow, heavy chords. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Tight Rhythm**: Modern, with the gain past its middle and the bass held back: some 3 dB less under 100 Hz than with the knobs at noon, for fast, muted playing. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Vintage Rhythm**: Vintage, where the power stage's feedback evens the lows and the top out, with the presence at its top to let the highs through: more middle and less boom than modern. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
