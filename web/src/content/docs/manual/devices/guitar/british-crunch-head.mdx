**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the early-1980s British master-volume valve head of hard rock.

A modelled British master-volume guitar head of one channel, without a cabinet: an input valve, a cold-biased second stage that clips one side of the wave first, a third stage into a cathode follower, a passive tone stack whose knobs lean on each other, a master before the phase inverter, and four European output pentodes in push-pull on a speaker's load, with the presence in the power stage's feedback loop. Preamp, bass, middle, treble, presence, and master, a high input and a low one that passes the input valve by, and a quality switch for how far above the sample rate the circuit is solved. It wants a cabinet after it, the speaker cabinet device. Modelled on the British 100-watt master-volume lead head of the early 1980s, the crunch and lead sound of that decade's hard rock.

## Presets

8 factory presets come with British Crunch Head. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Classic Crunch**: The head's crunch: the preamp just past its middle and the middle up, for open chords and riffs. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Clean, Low Input**: The low input, which passes the input valve by, with the preamp and the master at their tops: clean played softly, a little hair when dug into, and some 10 dB quieter than the others. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Cranked Master**: The preamp at its middle and the master far up, so the power stage clips too and squeezes the chords. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Dark Blues**: A light crunch with the treble and the presence down: a warm, round top for blues. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Edge of Breakup**: The high input with the preamp low: chords that clean up with the guitar's volume and bite when hit hard. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Hot Lead**: The preamp at its top and the middle well up, for single notes that sustain. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Rock Rhythm**: More preamp than the crunch and a little less bass: tight power chords. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
- **Scooped Eighties**: The preamp high, the bass and treble up and the middle down: the scooped rhythm of the decade. Put a cabinet after it, such as Boxed Twelves.
