**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the early-1980s Japanese bass machine whose accent and slide became the sound of acid.

A modelled acid bass line synthesizer of the early 1980s, built from its circuit and fitted to a reference by measurement: one oscillator, its saw or the square a waveshaper makes from it, into a four-pole diode-ladder low-pass and an amplifier. A decay-only envelope on the filter by its amount and decay, and a fixed one on the amplifier. Played by notes: a velocity of 100 or more accents a note, louder and brighter, accents in a row building through the accent's sweep; a note struck while another is held slides to its pitch. A drive after it: off, or the overdrive or the distortion pedal's circuit.

### Bass

- **Accent Builder**: The resonance and the accent at their tops and little envelope, so plain notes stay dark and accents leap: play accents in a row and each one sings higher than the last.
- **Bright Acid**: The filter higher and the resonance strong: a bright line that cuts through a full mix.
- **Chirp**: The envelope at its top with a short decay and the resonance high: each step a quick chirp falling from the top.
- **Classic Squelch**: The saw through a resonant filter that the envelope snaps open and lets fall: the squelching line the instrument is known for. Accented notes bark and whistle, and accents in a row build.
- **Dark Rumble**: The saw an octave down, the filter almost shut and a long decay, through the overdrive: a dark, growling rumble.
- **Deep Bass**: The square through a filter low and nearly closed, with little envelope and no resonance: a round, deep bass that sits under a mix. Accents only lean on it.
- **Distorted Square**: The square squelch into the distortion pedal's circuit, its tone dark: a fat, buzzing line.
- **Fuzz Bass**: The saw through a filter left fairly open with little resonance, into the distortion pedal's circuit: a fuzz bass for patterns that want weight more than squelch.
- **Hollow Square**: The square through a filter left open with a little resonance and envelope: a hollow, reedy bass that keeps its edges.
- **Low Acid**: The squelch an octave down, the filter a little lower: a deep acid line that is half bass.
- **Muted Acid**: The filter low and the decay short, the resonance up: dark, ticking steps that only the accents open.
- **Open Acid**: A long decay and a lot of envelope, so the filter barely closes between steps: a washy, open acid line for long builds.
- **Overdriven Square**: The square through a resonant filter into the overdrive pedal's circuit, which thickens its middle: a warm, gritty bass.
- **Plucked Blip**: The shortest decay and the envelope at its top, through a resonant filter left low: each note a short, plucked blip.
- **Rave Acid**: The saw with the resonance and envelope high into the overdrive pedal's circuit: a hard, singing line for fast tracks.
- **Rolling Saw**: The saw with a short decay and a middling resonance: tight sixteenths that roll along, each step clicking and gone before the next.
- **Rubber Bass**: The square through a half-resonant filter that pops open at each note and settles quickly: a bouncy, rubbery bass.
- **Screaming Acid**: The squelch at full resonance and envelope, driven hard into the distortion pedal's circuit: a screaming, distorted acid line whose accents tear.
- **Slide Line**: A long decay and a resonant filter that stays open through tied notes, for lines played legato: every note struck before the last one ends glides into its pitch without striking the envelope again.
- **Soft Acid**: The saw through a filter with half the resonance and a little envelope: a gentle, rounded acid line for quieter tracks.
- **Square Squelch**: The square through the filter near its singing, snapped open at each step: a hollow, rubbery squelch.
- **Steady Acid**: Little accent and a moderate resonance: an even line in which every step sounds alike, for patterns that carry their own shape.

### Lead

- **High Acid**: The squelch an octave up, bright and singing, sliding where the notes tie: an acid lead over a bass line.
- **Resonant Lead**: The saw an octave up, the resonance nearly at its top and a long decay, so the filter sings over each note: a whistling lead that glides where notes overlap.
- **Whistle Acid**: The square an octave up with the resonance at its top and a long decay: the filter whistles over every step, the accents shrieking.
