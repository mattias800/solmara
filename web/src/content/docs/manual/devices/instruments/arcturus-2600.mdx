**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the early-1970s American semi-modular studio synth, its modules patched on the panel and played from a duophonic keyboard.

A modelled semi-modular monosynth of the early 1970s, built from its circuit and held to a reference by measurement, its normalled routing the default patch: three oscillators (saws, a square, a triangle, a sine and pulses, sync and frequency modulation between them), noise with its colour, a ring modulator, a four-pole low-pass to singing, an ADSR and an AR, an amplifier with linear and exponential inputs, a mixer with pan and spring reverb, a sample-and-hold on its clock, an envelope follower and a lag. The common patch cords are panel choices. One voice, or two on the keyboard's lower and upper keys; portamento and delayed vibrato.

## Presets

41 factory presets come with Arcturus 2600. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

### Bass

- **Glide Bass**: VCO-1's saw (through the ring modulator, its second input off) and VCO-3's a little apart, gliding: a fat, sliding bass.
- **Pluck Bass**: VCO-3's saw through a resonant filter struck open and falling to nothing: a plucked, twanging bass.
- **Ring Bass**: VCO-1's saw ringing against VCO-2's sine a fifth up, under its square: a hollow, metallic bass.
- **Rubber Bass**: The square through a resonant filter that follows the keys, struck hard and closing onto the note itself: a rubbery, squelching bass.
- **Square Bass**: VCO-1's square, the one ADSR on both the filter and the amplifier, each note falling a little as it is held: a round, plain bass, the panel's own way.
- **Sub Bass**: VCO-1's square with VCO-2's pulse an octave under it, the filter low and barely moving: a deep, solid sub.
- **Sync Bass**: VCO-2 synced to VCO-1 a fifth up, the ADSR sweeping its pitch down as it falls: a tearing sync bass.
- **Thump Bass**: The square with a burst of noise as the filter opens and shuts: a bass with a thump at each note.

### Bell

- **Ring Bell**: VCO-1's saw fully ringed against VCO-2 an octave and a fifth up and a little sharp, struck and left to fade: a struck metal bell that rings on the key.

### Brass

- **Brass**: Two saws, the filter opening as the attack swells in: a single brass voice.

### Drone

- **Space Drone**: All three oscillators slowly apart, VCO-3 an octave down, a resonant filter, a slow swell and the spring: a long drone in space.

### Duo

- **Duo Brass**: Two voices of brass, the lowest and the highest of each chord, swelling in together.
- **Duo Lead**: The keyboard's two voices: the lower key on VCO-1's square, the upper on VCO-2's pulse, so a held interval plays both.

### FX

- **Falling Tone**: VCO-1 thrown three octaves up and falling slowly back while the key is held: a bomb's whistle.
- **Laser**: The ADSR striking VCO-1's pitch three octaves up and dropping at once: a laser shot at each key.
- **Siren**: VCO-3 in the low range as a slow LFO, its saw sweeping VCO-1 up over and over: an alarm siren.
- **Surf**: White noise swelling in over a second and dying away slowly through the spring: a wave on a beach.
- **Thunder**: Dark noise struck through a low filter and rumbling away into the spring: a thunderclap.
- **Trill**: VCO-3's pulse at a few hertz flicking VCO-1 up a tone and back: a trill under a held key.
- **Wind**: Pink noise through a resonant filter swept slowly by VCO-3's saw, swelling in: wind.

### Init

- **Init**: The panel as it comes: VCO-1's square through the filter half open, the ADSR on the filter and the amplifier.

### Lead

- **Classic Lead**: All three oscillators a little apart through a filter that follows the keys, the vibrato coming in after a moment: the instrument's own singing lead.
- **Glide Lead**: Two saws a few cents apart sliding from note to note, a slow vibrato: a smooth portamento lead.
- **Octave Lead**: The square under VCO-2's narrow pulse an octave up: a hollow, reedy lead in octaves.
- **Ring Lead**: VCO-1's saw ringing against VCO-2 an octave and a fifth up: a clangorous, bell-edged lead.
- **Screaming Lead**: The square and VCO-3's saw through a filter near singing, a little of the spring: a screaming solo voice.
- **Sync Lead**: VCO-2 synced to VCO-1 an octave up, the ADSR sweeping it down at each note: a bright, tearing sync lead.
- **Whistle**: No oscillator at all: the filter singing at middle C and following the keys, a touch of vibrato: a pure, whistled line.

### Percussion

- **Closed Hat**: Bright noise through the filter at its top, a short decay: a closed hi-hat.
- **Kick**: The filter singing low with nothing through it, the ADSR striking it a few octaves up and letting it fall: a round, deep kick.
- **Metal Hit**: Two oscillators an out-of-tune tritone apart through the ring modulator, struck and gone: a clanging metal hit.
- **Snare**: White noise through the filter opened by a short strike: a dry, bright snare.
- **Tom**: The sung filter higher, struck less far and following the keys an octave an octave: a tom tuned as it is played.
- **Zap Drum**: The sung filter struck four octaves up and dropping fast: an electronic zap of a drum.

### Pluck

- **Spring Pluck**: A short square pluck thrown into the spring at both sides: the tank's own splash after every note.

### Sample & Hold

- **Bubbles**: Random steps into the filter singing with nothing through it, the spring after it: bubbles rising in water.
- **Computer Chatter**: The noise sampled twelve times a second, two octaves deep into VCO-1's pitch: a film computer thinking aloud.
- **Random Burble**: The noise sampled ten times a second into the filter's third input, a resonant square under it: the classic burbling random filter.
- **Random Steps**: The noise sampled five times a second into VCO-1's and VCO-2's pitch: random notes stepping under a held key.
- **Robot Talk**: Random steps in VCO-1's pitch rung against VCO-2's sine, with a little resonance: a small robot chattering.
- **Staircase**: VCO-3's slow saw sampled by a fast clock into the filter: its sweep climbs as a staircase, over and over.
