**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the late-1970s Japanese flagship polysynth with two layers to a voice, a ribbon and pressure on every key.

A modelled eight-voice polysynth of the late 1970s whose every voice is two whole synthesizers layered, held to a reference by measurement: each channel a VCO with its saw, its square and its pulse-width LFO, noise and a sine beside the filters, a high-pass and a low-pass in series each with its resonance, a filter envelope from its initial level to its attack level, and an ADSR. The strike brightens, raises and bends each note; each key's own pressure brightens and raises it and drives the sub-oscillator's speed, vibrato and filter swing on it. Keyboard control of brilliance and level, a sub-oscillator, a ring modulator with its own envelope, detune, portamento or glissando, a ribbon, and a chorus or tremolo.

### Bass

- **Layered Bass**: Square at 16' under saw at 8', a short resonant filter envelope, the chorus spreading it; harder notes brighter.
- **Pluck Bass**: A saw through a resonant low-pass flicked open and shut by each note over a square an octave down: a round, plucked bass with a squelch on the hard notes.
- **Sub Bass**: Channel I's sine at 16' under channel II's square at 8' through a nearly closed filter, quick and still: a round, dark sub-bass.
- **Unison Bass**: Saws at 8' and 16', channel II pulled sharp so the two beat against each other, the filters opening a little on each note and more on the hard ones, dry: a thick, rolling bass.

### Bell

- **Celesta**: Pure sines at 4' and 2', struck and quickly gone, a gentle tremolo: a celesta, or a music box, up high.
- **Glass Chime**: The sines of both channels at 4' and 2' through the ring modulator, struck and ringing away up high: a cold, glassy chime.
- **Gong Bell**: A square and a sine through the ring modulator, its speed thrown up by each strike and snapping back, so each stroke starts with a clangorous slide before it rings: a gong-like, metallic bell.
- **Thin Chime**: A saw through the high-pass and the low-pass set together into a narrow, resonant band, struck and ringing: a thin, whistling chime, as in the film scores that made the instrument famous. After Reverb Machine's walkthrough of the instrument.
- **Tubular Bells**: A sine and a saw through the ring modulator, each strike throwing its speed up into a clangour that clears at once, leaving the tone beating slowly as it rings for seconds, harder strikes louder: tubular bells. After Sound On Sound's Synth Secrets on bells.
