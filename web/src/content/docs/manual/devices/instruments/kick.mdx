**A design of Solmara's own.**

A kick drum of five layers, each a body (a sine blended with a waveform and a wavetable), a click, noise, or a sample or factory click, shaped over the hit by curves of pitch, level, and place in the table, with points that follow the key and lock the phase; velocity, random, and LFO, drive, tone, and a limiter. Its layers go to bus A, bus B, or straight on, and its chains busA, busB, and master hold effects for each, the master's for what they come to.

[Kick on the website](/solmara/instruments/kick/)

## Presets

20 factory presets come with Kick. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Deep House Round**: After Beat Kitchen's drum design guide: a gentler sweep that thumps rather than snaps, the body gone by 300 ms, a felt beater, a little valve warmth and the top rolled off at 3.5 kHz, tuned low to F sharp.
- **Dusty Tape**: After Tim Cant's boom bap walkthrough: a round kick through a sampler's 6.2 kHz rate at 10 bits, warmed by valve drive, a crunchy click and a little hiss; each hit a little different in pitch and length.
- **Eight-O Boom Long**: The same machine's boom with its decay turned up, as Bonedo's walkthrough sets it (a second for the long one), tuned to G as Deruty and colleagues measured the machine's samples; it goes a little flat as it dies away, as Sound On Sound's bass drum article says the circuit does at long decays. Its root key is G1, where it plays as tuned; its tail follows the key, so C plays C.
- **Eight-O Boom Short**: After the classic drum machine's boom as MusicRadar's analogue-kick walkthrough tunes it (to 56 Hz, as its manual says) and Geoff Smith's Sound On Sound recreation: a sine a few semitones sharp at the strike falling to A in 30 ms, its level dying away on its own over half a second, and the trigger's short tick through a dark filter.
- **Gabber Crush**: After a hardcore producers' tutorial on the 1990s gabber kick: the classic machine's kick overdriven by 30 dB until it is a square wave, darkened at 7 kHz, harder still at full velocity.
- **Hard Tok**: After Music Production Wiki's hardstyle kick walkthrough: a tok of 15 ms at 150 Hz over a body that falls from C5 to C2 in 120 ms and holds its note, the tail clipped hard and tuned to the key; the drive lets go at its end so it stops cleanly.
- **House Classic**: After Computer Music's house kick walkthrough: half a second long, a little triangle in the body, driven a little, a plastic beater for its click; the sweep from 170 Hz settles on A.
- **Jazz Felt**: An open, tuned-up drum struck with felt: the skin's first modes ringing at 1.59, 2.14, 2.30 and 2.65 times its note, as a stretched membrane's do, over a little low noise for the shell, half a second long.
- **Long Boom**: A long sub that holds its note: play it on several keys and its tail plays the line.
- **Minimal Tick**: After Attack Magazine's minimal kick walkthrough, which takes the decay down to 60 or 70 ms for techno and minimal: a tiny body and a bright blip, gone in a tenth of a second.
- **Nine-O Punch**: After the 1980s drum machine's kick as Sound On Sound's bass drum article and Seedj's walkthrough describe it: a body near a sine, a little triangle left in, thrown from 210 Hz down to 50 Hz in 50 ms, and the attack a short pulse over low-passed noise.
- **Rock Kick**: After Gordon Reid's Synth Secrets on the bass drum: a skin whose pitch falls by a tenth, its modes at 1.86, 2.72 and 3.58 times its note, a burst of noise around 700 Hz for the shell, and a plastic beater; ported, so it is short.
- **Roller**: After ModeAudio's drum synth walkthrough for drum and bass: a sine at 58 Hz whose pitch falls in about 50 ms, short, with its knock lifted by two harmonics and a click near 4 kHz to cut through at 174 BPM.
- **Rumble Feeder**: A short, dry kick to feed a rumble, as Future Music's walkthrough builds one: send it to a fully wet reverb, distort that and low-pass it near 100 Hz, and duck it with the kick. Its tick cuts through the rumble; its body is gone by 200 ms so the rumble fills the gap.
- **Saturated Trap Sub**: After Audeobox's trap walkthrough: a long sub falling two octaves into its note, saturated by 7 dB so its harmonics carry it on small speakers, and rounded off at 6.5 kHz; its tail follows the key from D sharp, a key many trap producers write in, so the note you play is the note you hear.
- **Sub Line**: A kick to play bass lines with: the whole hit follows the key from F, so the note you play is the note you hear, a short drop of five semitones for its front, and the note held while the key is down and cut when it is let go. Two quiet harmonics keep it heard on small speakers.
- **Table Growl**: A body half of the harmonic build, bright at the strike and pure in the tail, through valve drive, with the plastic beater over it.
- **Techno Punch**: After myloops' hard techno kick walkthrough: the body thrown from two and a half octaves over its note to G in 35 ms, a second harmonic for the punch in the middle, a 10 ms band of noise at 5 kHz for its click, and hard clipping.
- **Warehouse Drive**: A techno kick through valve drive, darkened at 5.5 kHz, with a plastic beater; the drive lets go over its last quarter so it dies away rather than stopping.
- **Zap Psy**: A short, tight kick for rolling basslines, a little triangle in its body and the zap over its start.
