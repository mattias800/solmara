**An emulation of a digital original,** modelled on the first commercial digital reverb, of the mid-1970s.

The first digital reverb, of the mid-1970s, modelled as its algorithm: a long loop of memory with many taps between converters of a short word with gain ranging, at a low sample rate behind steep filters, so the tail is dense, smooth, and dark above 11 kHz. A time of 0.4 to 4.5 s, with the lows and the highs at multiples of it, and pre-delay; beside the reverb, its programs of two delays, phasing, chorus, echo, and a long space. The converters' hiss, mix, and output. Edits never stop the tail.

## Presets

10 factory presets come with 1976 Digital Reverb. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

### Delay

- **Falling Echo**: The echo program every 235 ms, the high lever at half so each repeat is 2.5 dB under the last, turned over.
- **Wide Slap**: The delay program: 80 ms on the left and 120 on the right, each a single repeat, widening a vocal or a guitar.

### Modulation

- **Four Voice Chorus**: The chorus program's third mode: two delays a side wandering at random, a thick, wide doubling.
- **Lever Phasing**: The phasing program at 3.2 ms: a short delay against a fixed one, a comb of notches that the lever moves when it is turned or automated.

### Reverb

- **As It Starts**: The unit as it is switched on: 2.8 seconds, the lows as long as the middle, the highs at half, 40 ms before it. The dense, smooth, dark tail of 1970s and 1980s records, for vocals, strings, and a mix's send.
- **Dark Cathedral**: The longest time with the lows doubled and the highs at a quarter: a deep, dark tail for pads and slow parts.
- **Endless Space**: The space program: some ten seconds at every frequency, as bright as the converters allow. A wash under a drone or a pad.
- **Small Room**: Under a second, the highs at half: a small, dark room around drums or a guitar that does not wash them.
- **Snare Bloom**: A short, dense burst for a snare: 1.2 seconds with the lows doubled and the highs at a third, no gap.
- **Vocal Tail**: Two seconds with the highs open to the converters' edge and 20 ms before it, so a voice stays in front of its tail.
