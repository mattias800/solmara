**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the 1970s British class-A console preamp and equaliser.

A modelled console channel of the class-A kind, for shaping single tracks: a line transformer, a stepped gain switch, an amplifier of three transistors, an equaliser of switched frequencies, and an output amplifier whose last transistor drives a gapped output transformer. A shelf at 12 kHz, a presence band at 360 Hz to 7.2 kHz on coils, a low shelf at 35, 60, 110, or 220 Hz, and a high-pass of 18 dB an octave at 50, 80, 160, or 300 Hz, with switches for the equaliser and the phase and a trim. Turned up, the output stage runs out of current and its transformer fills on low notes, so the lows thicken and distort first, mostly in the second and third harmonics. In stereo, each channel through its own circuits, with a quality switch for how far above the sample rate they are solved. Modelled on the British microphone and line amplifier with equaliser of the early 1970s, built on discrete class-A amplifier cards between transformers.
