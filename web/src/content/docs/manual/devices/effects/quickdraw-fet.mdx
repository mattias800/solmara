**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the late-1960s American FET limiting amplifier and its all-buttons mode.

A modelled peak limiter built on a field-effect transistor: the transistor is the lower leg of a divider at the input and turns the audio down as the sidechain, which listens after it, lifts its gate. It has no threshold knob: the input knob drives the audio into a fixed threshold and the output knob makes up the level. Attack and release, both fastest at the top of their travel, with an attack from a few milliseconds down to a tenth of one, and a button for each ratio, 4, 8, 12, or 20 to 1, none of them, or all four at once, which pumps and distorts. It can listen to another track by sidechain. Modelled on the black-faced American transistor limiting amplifier of the late 1960s, the usual fast, aggressive compressor for drums, vocals, and bass.

## Presets

10 factory presets come with Quickdraw FET. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Bass, Pinned**: 8 to 1 with the slowest attack and a slow release, so that the reduction does not follow the wave of a low note and bend it. Keeps every note of a bass at one level.
- **Drum Bus, Smash**: 20 to 1 with both timing knobs at their fastest and the input well up: everything flattened. Meant to be mixed in under the drums as they are, from a return track.
- **Drum Room, All Buttons**: All four buttons in with the release at its fastest: the room between the hits is pulled up and pumps, with the distortion that goes with it. For a room or an overhead track, or a copy of the drums mixed in under them.
- **Guitar, Evened**: 4 to 1 at a middling attack and a quicker release, for a clean or an acoustic guitar whose picked notes jump out.
- **Iron Only**: No button in: nothing is turned down, and what is left is the amplifiers and the transformers, which thicken what is driven into them. Turn the input up and the output down for more of it.
- **Kick, Punch**: 4 to 1 with a slow attack and a release of a quarter of a second or so: the beater passes and the body is evened out.
- **Peak Catcher**: 20 to 1 with the fastest attack and the input low: nothing is touched but what rises above some -8 dBFS, which is held there within a tenth of a millisecond.
- **Snare, Crack**: 4 to 1, the attack at its slowest, which still is a few milliseconds, and the release at its fastest: the stick passes, the ring after it is brought up.
- **Vocal, Held Firm**: 8 to 1 at the middle of both timing knobs, for a voice that moves too much to sit in a dense mix.
- **Vocal, Up Front**: 4 to 1 with a slower attack and a quick release: a voice is held in place and its consonants come through. Turn the input until the meter moves 3 to 7 dB.
