**An emulation of a digital original,** modelled on the late-1970s American digital reverb whose halls and plates were on every record of the 1980s.

A digital hall reverb of the late 1970s, modelled as its algorithm: a network of delays and diffusers that recirculate at a low sample rate between steep converter filters, so the tail is dark above 8 kHz and grainy far down, with its delays wandering slowly so it never rings. Nine programs: two small halls, a large hall, a chamber, a room, three plates, and a chorus. Bass and mid decay times split at a crossover, a treble decay, depth (how far back the sound sits), pre-delay, diffusion, wander and chorus for the delays' motion, the converters' hiss, mix, and output. Edits never stop the tail.

## Presets

### Modulation

- **Chorus Wash**: The chorus program: a short cluster of wandering delays rather than a reverb. Widens and thickens a guitar or a synth.

### Reverb

- **Chamber**: The chamber: dense at once and warm, with no gap before it. For drums and guitars that want a room around them.
- **Constant Plate**: The constant plate: the same time at every frequency, short and dense. A plate that stays out of the way.
- **Eighties Snare Hall**: The small hall with a short middle and long lows, sent loud: the big snare of the mid-1980s.
- **Infinite Pad**: The large hall at sixteen seconds in the middle and longer below, its delays wandering slowly: a wash that hangs under a pad or a drone.
- **Large Hall**: The large hall: a bigger loop, a slower build, and a long dark tail for pads, ballads, and orchestral parts.
- **Percussion Plate**: The percussion plate: diffused hard so a snare or a clap blooms at once into a smooth wash.
- **Room**: The room: under two seconds, its top falling early. Puts drums and percussion in a space without washing them.
- **Small Hall A**: The first small hall as the unit loads it: three seconds in the bass, two in the middle, dark above 6.6 kHz. The digital hall of 1980s records, for vocals, strings, and a mix's send.
- **Small Hall B**: The second small hall: its treble shorter and its diffusion lower, so the tail is darker and its first echoes more distinct.
- **Vocal Plate**: The vocal plate: bright to the converters' edge and even across the bands, with no gap; the plate sound for a lead vocal.
