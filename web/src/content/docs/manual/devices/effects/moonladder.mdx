**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the four-pole transistor ladder filter of the late-1960s American modular and portable synths.

A modelled transistor ladder low-pass filter, the one in the classic American three-oscillator portable monosynth of the early 1970s, as an effect: an input pair, four transistor pairs stacked over it with a capacitor across each, an output amplifier, and the emphasis fed from the output back to the input pair. Its four poles fall 24 dB an octave; emphasis thins the bass, peaks the corner, and near the top of its travel sings by itself; drive pushes the input pair into its curve, warm and thick on the second harmonic. Cutoff from -5 to 5 as the instrument's panel has it, emphasis and drive from 0 to 10, an output, and a light quality at about half the processing.

## Presets

8 factory presets come with Moonladder. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Hot Input**: Drive near its top: the input pair is pushed deep into its curve, thick and buzzing on the second and third harmonics, and the output turned down to match.
- **Open Warmth**: Wide open, the input pair driven a little: nothing taken away, a touch of second harmonic and the couplings' gentle low cut. For a synth or a drum bus that wants the ladder's colour without its filtering.
- **Round Bass**: Cutoff low, no emphasis: the four poles roll off the top of a bass line at 24 dB an octave and leave its body.
- **Singing Corner**: Emphasis at the top: the ladder sings a sine at its corner once something starts it, and a note through it is pulled and beaten against. Turn the cutoff to play it.
- **Squelch**: Emphasis high and cutoff in the low middle: the corner peaks hard and the bass thins under it, the vowel of a plucked monosynth bass. Move the cutoff for the sweep.
- **Telephone Dark**: Cutoff near the bottom of its travel: only the lows pass, as from a room away.
- **Thin Brass**: The corner in the upper middle with moderate emphasis: the bass thinned a little and a peak near 500 Hz, a brassy edge for a saw.
- **Vocal Peak**: The corner a little above 1 kHz, emphasis at six: a narrow peak that gives a lead line a voice.
