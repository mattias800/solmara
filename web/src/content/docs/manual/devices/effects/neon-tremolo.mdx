**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the optical tremolo of the 1960s American guitar amplifiers.

A modelled optical tremolo of the American amplifier kind, for guitars, electric pianos, and organs: a valve oscillator drives a neon lamp sealed with a photocell, and the cell turns the signal down as the lamp lights it. The lamp flashes once a swing, the intensity setting how brightly, and the cell darkens fast and lets go slowly, so the throb is not a sine: it falls in a few milliseconds and climbs back over tens of them. Speed and intensity, as the amplifier has, and an output. Both sides through one cell. Modelled on the tremolo of the black-panel American combos of the mid-1960s.

## Presets

6 factory presets come with Neon Tremolo. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Fast Stutter**: The speed knob near its top with the intensity full: as fast as the cell can follow, which is why it is shallower than slower settings.
- **Gentle Shimmer**: A low intensity: the lamp burns dimly and the level dips by a couple of decibels once a swing. A movement felt more than heard.
- **Slow Pulse**: The speed knob at its bottom, some 3.7 Hz, and fairly deep, for chords left to ring.
- **Surf Throb**: Deep and choppy at some 5 Hz: the throb of the surf records, the level falling fast as the lamp flashes and climbing back slowly.
- **Swamp Shuffle**: Slow, under a shuffle, the intensity at three quarters: the tremolo of the swamp-blues and roots records.
- **Tine Piano Throb**: A tine piano through an amplifier's tremolo, at a medium speed and moderate depth.
