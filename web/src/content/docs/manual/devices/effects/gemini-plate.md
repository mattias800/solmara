**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the late-1950s German studio plate: a sheet of steel in a frame two metres long.

A plate reverberator modelled as a plate: a thin sheet of steel one metre by two, under tension, with one driver, two pickups for left and right, and a damping plate whose distance sets the decay. The sheet is some twenty-seven thousand modes of bending waves, each ringing at its own frequency and dying at its own rate, so the lows ring long and the highs short, the highs of a hit arrive before its lows, and the tail is dense from the start. Decay is the damper, from half a second to five and a half at low frequencies, and can be moved while the plate rings; pre-delay, low cut, high cut, width, mix, output, and a quality switch for a lighter plate with fewer modes. Both sides of the audio drive the one driver. Modelled on the large German studio plate reverberator of the late 1950s, the usual reverb for vocals and snare drums.
