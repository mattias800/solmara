**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the early-1960s American outboard spring reverb tank of surf guitar.

A spring reverb tank of the kind built into guitar amplifiers and sold as studio units, modelled as its springs: two or three long coil springs, each driven at one end and heard at the other, each a bank of modes of one kind of wave whose speed falls as its pitch rises, so a hit comes out as a chirp, lows first, and comes back as a longer one each time it has been to the far end and back: the drip and the boing of surf guitar. Dwell drives the valve amplifier that drives the tank, and a loud transient overdrives it into a crash; tone is a shelf on what comes back. Decay, a short or a long tank, two or three springs panned across the sides, pre-delay, width, mix, and output. No latency.

## Presets

8 factory presets come with Drip Spring. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Crashing Tank**: The dwell all but full: the drive amplifier clips on every loud note and the tank crashes and splashes, as when an amplifier is kicked. For a dramatic hit, or to automate up for one bar.
- **Dub Spring**: A long, dark tank held back 60 ms, all wet, for a return track: send a snare or a skank chord to it in bursts and let it boing.
- **Keys Spring**: Three springs, wide and a little bright, at a moderate dwell: an electric piano or an organ in a room of coils.
- **Short Tank Slap**: The short tank with two springs and a short decay: a tight, metallic bounce behind a snare or a percussive part.
- **Studio Spring Send**: The long tank alone with the dwell at its middle, for a return track: the springs as a studio unit was used, clean unless a loud hit drives it.
- **Subtle Amp Spring**: The short tank turned low behind a clean or crunchy amplifier, as most amplifiers' reverb knob is left: a little space and a little bounce, not heard as an effect.
- **Surf Drip**: The long tank with three springs, the dwell well up and the tone bright: every picked note drips and a hard one crashes the springs. For surf and twang guitar, on the amplifier's clean channel.
- **Vocal Spring**: Two springs, darker, held back 25 ms so the words come first: the boxy, bouncing reverb of 1960s records behind a voice.
