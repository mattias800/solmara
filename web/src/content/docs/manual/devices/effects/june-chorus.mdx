**An emulation of an analogue original,** modelled on the chorus of the early-1980s Japanese six-voice polysynths.

A modelled bucket-brigade chorus, for synths, pads, and clean guitars: two delay lines of 256 stages, each a chain of capacitors that a clock passes the signal along, so that the delay is the clock's rate. One triangle sweeps the two clocks in opposite directions, one line's delay growing as the other's shrinks, and the dry signal is mixed beside them, which spreads a mono sound wide. Two buttons, as the unit has: I sweeps slowly, II faster, and I and II together is a quick, shallow vibrato in both lines at once. The lines sample what comes in at their clock, so their images are heard on high notes, and their transfer adds a second harmonic and a little third. Rate and depth against the mode's own, a mix, a width, a stereo or summed input, drive, the lines' noise, and an output. Modelled on the chorus built into the Japanese six-voice polysynths of the early 1980s.

## Presets

8 factory presets come with June Chorus. Click the device's name to browse them, and try one while the song plays; the library has them too. See [presets](/solmara/manual/guides/devices/#presets).

- **Clean Guitar Shimmer**: The second button a little slower and deeper, a touch less wet: a clean electric guitar's arpeggios spread across the speakers and shimmer, as the chorus pedals of the same years did.
- **Fast Vibrato**: Both buttons together: a quick, shallow wobble in both lines at once, more vibrato than chorus, and with the mix at the wet end a pitch vibrato on its own. For organ-like and electric-piano patches.
- **Hissy Original**: The first button with the lines' own hiss, as an unrestored unit has it, and a little more drive into them for their second harmonic: the old chorus with its faults.
- **Narrow Bass Chorus**: The slow sweep with the width at a third and less wet: movement on a synth bass that stays in the middle of the mix, so the low end does not go wide.
- **Seasick**: The slow sweep at twice its depth and a little slower, all wet: the pitch bends up and down in opposite directions on the two sides, a detuned, tape-like wobble.
- **Strings II**: The second button: the faster sweep, which thickens a string patch or a brass stab into an ensemble without sounding seasick.
- **Subtle Widening**: The slow sweep at half its depth and a quarter of the mix: a mono source is given width and a little air without being heard as chorus. For a vocal double, a mono synth, or a bus.
- **Synth Pad I**: The first button as the unit has it: the slow sweep, half dry and half wet, the two lines moving against each other. The wide, gently moving pad of the early 1980s polysynths, on a sawtooth or a square.
