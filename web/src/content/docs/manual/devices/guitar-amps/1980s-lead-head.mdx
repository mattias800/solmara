**An emulation of an analog original,** modeled on the late-1980s British 100-watt two-channel lead head, its lead channel clipped through a pair of diodes.

[1980s Lead Head on the website](/solmara/guitar/#1980s-lead-head)

1980s Lead Head is a two-channel valve guitar head in the image of the British 100-watt lead heads of the late 1980s: the amplifier whose lead channel clips through a pair of diodes, a tight, saturated, singing sound that hard rock and metal players made their own, and whose rhythm channel stays nearly clean until you pull in its clip. It is built from the circuit: the valves, the diodes, the tone stack and the power stage, solved as the music plays.

It is a head alone, with no speaker: by itself it sounds harsh and fizzy, as a real head played straight into a desk does. **Put Boxed Twelves after it** for the cabinet and the microphone.

## At a glance

- **Two channels**: **Lead**, clipped through a pair of diodes, and **Rhythm**, nearly clean.
- **Rhythm Clip**: lifts the rhythm channel onto the same diodes, for a crunch.
- **Input Gain** for both channels, **Lead Master** for the lead channel's level, **Output Master** for the whole head.
- **A passive tone stack** (Bass, Middle, Treble) after the clipping, and **Presence** in the power stage.
- **Four output valves** in push-pull, driven by the Output Master.

The input valve and its **Input Gain** feed a second stage, and from there the two channels part. The lead channel clips through a red LED one way and silicon diodes the other, so it clips a little differently on each side of the wave, then passes the **Lead Master**. The rhythm channel takes the same stage through a pad to ground: clean, or, with the clip, through a pair of the same diodes. Either then drives a third stage and a cathode follower into the tone stack, and the **Output Master** sets how hard the power stage is driven.

## The controls

### CHANNEL

**LEAD** and **RHYTHM**. The lead channel is the head's lead sound: the preamp clips on its diodes, saturated and smooth. The rhythm channel comes from an earlier stage, nearly clean and some 16 dB quieter, and a little brighter. You can switch while you play: the two cross over in a few milliseconds.

### RHYTHM CLIP

**CLEAN** and **CLIP**. Clip lifts the rhythm channel some 7 dB onto a pair of the same diodes: a crunch for chords, as much a boost as a clipper. It reaches the lead channel too: with the clip on, the lead is some 3.5 dB quieter.

### Presence

The top of the sound, in what the power stage feeds back. Turn it up to bring a dull cabinet forward; down for a darker, rounder lead.

### Bass, Middle and Treble

The passive tone stack, after the clipping. Its three knobs lean on each other, as on any amplifier of its kind: Middle low gives the scooped metal sound, Middle up a lead that cuts through a band. A right click on any of them offers **Reset the stack to noon**.

### Output Master

How hard the power stage is driven, and how loud the whole head is, on either channel: some 24 dB from 2 to 10. Turned up, the output valves add their own compression and grind.

### Lead Master

The lead channel's level after its diodes: some 28 dB from 1 to 10, silent at 0. It does nothing to the rhythm channel, so it is how the two channels are balanced: set the rhythm sound first, then bring the lead up to sit above it.

### Input Gain

How hard the preamp is driven, on both channels. On the lead channel it is the amount of saturation; on the rhythm channel it gives most of its gain below 7.5.

## Setting it up

1. Put **1980s Lead Head** on a guitar track, and **Boxed Twelves** after it.
2. Start on **LEAD** with everything at 5. Play, and set **Input Gain** for the saturation you want.
3. Shape the sound with the stack and **Presence**, with the cabinet on: alone, the head is always too bright.
4. Switch to **RHYTHM**, set its sound, then go back to **LEAD** and set **Lead Master** so the lead sits above it.
5. Set **Output Master** last, for the level and the power stage's push.

## Starting points

- **Classic lead.** LEAD, Input Gain 7, Bass 5, Middle 7, Treble 6, Presence 6, Lead Master 6.
- **Scooped metal rhythm.** LEAD, Input Gain 8, Bass 7, Middle 2, Treble 7, Presence 7.
- **Crunch chords.** RHYTHM with CLIP, Input Gain 6, Middle 6.
- **Clean.** RHYTHM, CLEAN, Input Gain 4, Treble 6, Presence 4.

## Tips

- **Always play it through a cabinet.** Boxed Twelves after it turns the fizz into a guitar sound.
- **Switch channels with automation.** Draw the Channel or Rhythm Clip in a lane, and the head changes sound where the song does, as a footswitch would.
- **Tame the fizz with Treble and Presence, not Input Gain.** The diodes keep their saturation as you lower the top.
