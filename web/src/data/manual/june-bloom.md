June Bloom is a six-voice polysynth with one oscillator a voice, a resonant low-pass, one envelope, one LFO and a stereo chorus. That is all it has, and it is enough for warm pads, round basses, brass stabs and bright chords; most of its character is the chorus. A new June Bloom plays a saw with the sub-oscillator under it, the filter half open, a short envelope and the chorus on I.

## How the sound is made

Each of the six voices runs the same path, left to right on the panel:

1. **DCO**, the oscillator: a saw, a pulse and a square an octave down, mixed with noise.
2. **VCF**, a four-pole low-pass, opened and closed by the envelope, the LFO and the key you play.
3. **VCA**, the amplifier, opened by the envelope or straight by the key.

The six voices are then summed and go through the **HPF**, a stepped high-pass, the **chorus** and the **volume**. The LFO and the envelope serve every voice: the LFO is one for all six, the envelope runs once for each voice.

## The panel, section by section

### LFO

One triangle LFO for all six voices. **LFO rate** sets its speed, from a slow swell to a fast flutter. **LFO delay** holds it back after you strike a key with none held, then lets it grow in, so a held note starts plain and gains its vibrato: set it near 4 for a string player's late vibrato. The LFO does nothing until you send it somewhere: to the pitch here, to the pulse width in the DCO, and to the cutoff in the VCF.

### DCO

- **Range** picks the octave: 16', 8' or 4'.
- **LFO to pitch** (LFO) is vibrato. A little (1 to 2) is vibrato; more is a siren.
- **Pulse width** (PWM) sets the pulse's width by hand, or, with **Pulse width by** on LFO (the LFO and MAN buttons), how far the LFO sweeps it. A slow sweep is the classic moving, chorused pulse.
- **Pulse** and **Saw** switch each wave on. Both on is brighter and thicker; the pulse alone is hollow.
- **Sub-oscillator** (SUB) adds a square an octave down: the weight under a bass.
- **Noise** adds hiss, for breath, wind and percussion.

### HPF

**High-pass** (FREQ) is the high-pass after the voices, in four steps. **0** lifts the bass, **1** is flat, **2** and **3** thin the sound, cutting under about 240 Hz and 770 Hz. Use 2 or 3 to keep a pad out of the way of the bass and kick.

### VCF

- **Cutoff** (FREQ) is how open the filter is. **Resonance** (RES) makes it ring at the cutoff; from about 8 it sings on its own.
- **Envelope to filter** (ENV) is how far the envelope opens it, and **Envelope polarity** (+ENV and -ENV) turns that upside down: Negative closes the filter as the envelope rises.
- **LFO to filter** (LFO) sweeps the cutoff with the LFO: a wah, or a slow breathing.
- **Key follow** (KYBD) opens the filter as you play higher. At 10 it follows the keys an octave an octave, so a singing filter plays in tune.

### VCA

**Amplifier by** (ENV and GATE) chooses what opens the amplifier: the **Envelope**, or the **Gate**, full on while a key is held, like an organ. **Amplifier level** (LEVEL) sets the voices' level before the chorus.

### ENV

**Attack**, **Decay**, **Sustain** and **Release**, one envelope a voice. It drives the amplifier (with the VCA on Envelope) and, by **Envelope to filter**, the cutoff. A slow attack and long release make a pad; no attack, a short decay and no sustain make a pluck.

### Chorus

**Chorus** has four settings, by its two buttons: off, **I** (a slow, wide sweep), **II** (the same depth, nearly twice as fast), and **I+II** (a fast, shallow shimmer, the same in both sides). **Chorus output** (MONO and ST) plays it in **Stereo** or folds it to **Mono**. Turn the chorus off to hear how plain the voice beneath it is; turn it back on for the sound June Bloom is known for.

### More: voices, glide, bender and output

The strip under the sliders holds what June Bloom has beside the original's panel.

- **Voices** (POLY 1 and POLY 2) chooses how keys find a voice. **Poly 1** gives each new key the next voice in turn, so a released note rings on under the next. **Poly 2** gives a key the voice let go last, cutting its release: tighter for a fast line.
- **Glide** slides each voice's pitch to its new key. In Poly 2 each note glides from the one before.
- **Bender** is the pitch-bend lever, from -1 to 1. **Bender to pitch**, **Bender to filter** and **Bender to LFO** (TO PITCH, TO FILTER, TO LFO) set how far it moves each; at 10 the pitch bends an octave.
- **Bender's push to LFO** (PUSH) brings in the LFO by hand, 0 to 1. Your keyboard's **mod wheel** pushes it too: put some **LFO to pitch** in, leave the LFO's delay at 0, and the wheel adds vibrato as you play.
- **Volume** is the output level.
- **Quality** is **Full** (the filter run at twice the rate, the default) or **Light**, which sounds almost the same and costs half the processing. Use Light when a project has many voices playing.

## Pictures you can play

The window's pictures are controls too:

- **The LFO's wave** grows in by its delay, so you see what the delay does.
- **The filter's curve**: drag its corner sideways for the cutoff and up and down for the resonance.
- **The envelope**: drag its corners for the attack, the decay with the sustain, and the release.
- **Right-click the envelope** to set it as a pluck, a pad or an organ's, or to reset it.

While it plays, a dot for each voice shows where it is on the envelope and on the filter's curve, the last key struck brightest, and the LFO shows where it is in its cycle.

## Playing it

- Six notes sound at once. A seventh key held with six down is not played.
- June Bloom does not answer velocity: every key plays at the same level, as on the original.
- The track's **pitch bend** moves the **Bender** lever, and the **mod wheel** pushes the LFO in (see above).
- Every control can be automated, and each change is heard on the notes already playing.

## Starting points

From a new June Bloom (its controls as they start):

- **Warm pad.** Attack 5, Decay 6, Sustain 8, Release 6. Cutoff 4.5, Envelope to filter 1. Pulse on with **Pulse width by** LFO, LFO rate 3, Pulse width 4. Chorus II.
- **Round bass.** Range 16', Sub-oscillator 8, Cutoff 3, Resonance 3, Envelope to filter 5. Attack 0, Decay 3.5, Sustain 2, Release 1.5. Chorus off, **Voices** on Poly 2, HPF 0.
- **Brass stab.** Saw only, Cutoff 3.5, Envelope to filter 6, Attack 1.5, Decay 4, Sustain 5, Release 2. Chorus I.
- **Pluck.** Cutoff 2.5, Envelope to filter 7, Key follow 7, Attack 0, Decay 3, Sustain 0, Release 3.
- **Organ.** **Amplifier by** Gate, Pulse on, Saw off, Pulse width 5, Cutoff 7, Chorus I+II.
- **Singing filter.** Resonance 9, Key follow 10, Noise 0, Saw off, Pulse off, Sub-oscillator 0: the filter itself is the tone, played in tune by the keys.

## Tips

- **The chorus is half the sound.** Before reaching for an effect, try I and II.
- **A thin pad sits better.** In a full mix, HPF 2 makes room for the bass.
- **One LFO for all voices** means a vibrato or a filter sweep moves the whole chord together, as the original did.
- **For more voices or a second layer**, put a second June Bloom on another track, or both in an instrument rack.
